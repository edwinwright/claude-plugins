## The problem

One paragraph. What is wrong, for whom, and what it costs them. No solutions.

## Who has it

The people who feel this day to day, by role. If the person requesting a solution is not the person with the problem, say so here — that gap predicts failure better than almost anything else.

## Evidence

What supports the claim above, and how strong each piece is.

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| | | Measured / Observed / Reported / Assumed |

Anything load-bearing that rests on *Assumed* is the first thing worth verifying.

## Cost of doing nothing

What happens if this is never built. Money, risk, staff time, customers lost — and whether it is growing, steady, or already absorbed.

If the honest answer is "not much", write that. A problem with no cost of inaction is a preference, and it should compete for budget as one.

## What has been tried

Previous attempts and why they did not stick. Failure is rarely technical, and the reason is usually the most useful thing in this document.

## Constraints

Fixed conditions the solution has to live inside — budget, deadline, platform, regulation, an existing contract, a decision already taken elsewhere.

A solution that has already been chosen goes here, as a fact about the situation. It is not an output of discovery.
